# Media Vertical Evaluation Framework

Brian's personal framework for deciding whether a media vertical is worth pursuing — for acquisitions, launches, or sales strategy. Not a formula, a filter.

## Five Factors

**1. Sponsor product value** — The dollar value of what a potential sponsor sells. Enterprise software at $100K/yr is a different universe from a $20 consumer product. High-ticket B2B is the target. This determines the ceiling on what sponsors will pay.

**2. Audience buying power** — Two sub-dimensions: authority (can they actually sign the check?) and relevance (are they likely buyers for what sponsors sell?). A newsletter to engineers and one to CTOs in the same industry can have identical size but 5-10x different CPMs purely because of signing authority.

**3. Sales cycle length** — How long it takes to close a sponsor. Shorter cycles multiply the value of the first two factors. A vertical where sponsors sign in 30 days beats one where procurement takes 6 months, all else equal. Also consider purchase frequency — recurring or annual decisions are better than once-in-a-decade capital equipment buys.

**4. Exclusivity / defensibility** — How else can the sponsor reach this audience? LinkedIn ads, trade shows, direct outreach — the more alternatives exist, the less pricing power you have even with great economics. When you are the only quality editorial voice in a space, sponsors pay a premium and stay. This is the multiplier on top of the other factors: early + authoritative in a thin-competition vertical is the best position.

**5. Volume of deals** — How many purchasing decisions are actively happening in this market per year? A vertical can look attractive on all other factors but fail if the total buyer universe is small and relationship-driven. Nuclear energy is the canonical example: product values are enormous, buyers have real authority, but there are only ~60 operating plants in the US and every vendor already has every buyer's cell number. Media adds no discovery value in a 60-person market. You need enough active buyers that sponsors genuinely can't reach them all directly — that's when a newsletter becomes the efficient channel.

## How to Apply

Score each potential vertical or acquisition target across all five dimensions. Compare relative scores — this is directional, not precise. Use it to rank and eliminate, not calculate.

Red flags: low-value consumer products, audiences with no purchasing authority, long procurement-driven sales cycles, crowded editorial landscape, small/relationship-saturated buyer markets.

Green flags: high-ticket B2B products, decision-maker audiences, short-moderate sales cycles, few or no quality editorial competitors, and a large enough active buyer pool that sponsors can't shortcut the media channel.

## Canonical Failure Case

Nuclear/energy: scores well on product value, audience authority, and exclusivity — but fails on volume. The universe of buyers is so small and already interconnected that sponsors have direct relationships with every relevant person. A newsletter creates no discovery value.
