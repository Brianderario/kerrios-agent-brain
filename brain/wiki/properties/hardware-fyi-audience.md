## Tier A — directional splits (Apollo-enriched sample, 2026-06-02)

⚠️ **Directional only.** Method: sampled ~1,200 subscribers from the Dec-2025 export, enriched via Apollo. **361 matched (~30% match rate).** The matched set **skews corporate / senior / US** because Apollo cannot resolve students, `.edu`, personal-Gmail, and many international subscribers — so it **undercounts** the IC / academic / international tail that the real list carries. Use these as "working/professional core" figures, not whole-list truth. First used in the H0024 Neural Concept reply (sent 2026-06-02).

**Seniority** (of 329 with a resolved seniority):
- ~52% individual contributor (entry / intern / senior engineer)
- ~21% manager / director / head
- ~28% VP / C-suite / founder / owner

**Geography** (of 361 geolocated):
- ~75% United States, ~25% international
- US concentration: California (#1, ~40% of US), then New York, Texas, Washington, Massachusetts — West-Coast-heavy
- Top international: India, UK, Canada, Germany, Australia, Italy

**Company size** (of 320 with a resolved headcount):
- ~44% startup (<50)
- ~18% small (50–249)
- ~10% mid (250–999)
- ~7% large (1k–5k)
- ~21% enterprise (5k+)
- Read: barbell — startup-heavy with a real enterprise tail.

**Industry** (Apollo tags, multi-count — qualitative, do not quote as exact %):
IT & services, mechanical/industrial engineering, electrical/electronic manufacturing, machinery, VC/PE, higher education, research, defense & space, semiconductors, aviation/aerospace, computer software, computer hardware. Apollo's tags undercount core hardware verticals (CE/auto/aero), so present industry as a named-vertical list, not percentages.
