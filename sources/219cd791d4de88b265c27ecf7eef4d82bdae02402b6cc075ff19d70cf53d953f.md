When a research sub-agent's summary supplies a fact that will carry weight in a draft — a Lead thesis, a headline, a "first/biggest/only" superlative, an attribution of who did what — re-fetch the primary source and confirm the exact claim before drafting on it. Do not let an agent summary stand as the citation for a load-bearing claim.

**Why:** In the S&W newsletter (2026-06-22), the writer's research agent characterized Radiant Nuclear's Feb 2026 DOE approval as "the first full-power test approval under the program." That became the Lead's spine ("Radiant was first cleared for a full-power test"). Checking primary sources directly, it was the *second* of three safety milestones, Westinghouse had completed the same phase, and Radiant had not operated a reactor at all. Antares (Jun 4) and Valar (Jun 18) were the actual criticalities. Brian caught the error. The scoreboard facts were right; the unverified superlative was not.

**How to apply:** The S&W writer routine already re-verifies the Lead spine — extend that to every superlative and every who-did-what attribution, not just dates and dollar figures. A research agent's "first" is a lead to verify, not a fact to print. This is the [[coding_standards]] "correctness is on me, verify don't assume" rule applied to research synthesis.
