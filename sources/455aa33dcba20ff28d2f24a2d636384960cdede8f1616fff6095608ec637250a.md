In ALL writing (emails, drafts, newsletters, chat, docs), never use the seven dead giveaways of AI writing. Source Brian gave me: medium.com/@jess_33150/7-dead-giveaways-of-ai-writing-df5145f13498.

The seven, and how to avoid each:
1. **Em-dash overuse.** Never use em dashes at all (already a hard rule, see [[feedback_no_emdashes]]). Use commas, colons, periods, or parentheses.
2. **The "it's not about X, it's about Y" / "no longer X, it's Y" / "not just X but Y" negation formula.** Banned outright. State the point directly and positively. (The line Brian killed on 2026-06-17: "the hard part is no longer the processor, it's getting all of them to act as one machine.")
3. **Rule-of-three with alliteration** ("clear, concise, and compelling"). Don't manufacture triplets for cadence. A list of three real items is fine; a rhetorical trio is not.
4. **Wikipedia voice** — balanced, comprehensive, please-everyone prose. Write with a point of view; be specific and opinionated.
5. **Clichéd metaphors** — "rich tapestry," "landscape of X," "in today's fast-paced world." One concrete, literal-adjacent metaphor max, never a stock one.
6. **Enthusiasm overload** — "exciting," "powerful," "revolutionary," "groundbreaking," "transformative." Cut hype adjectives; let the number carry it.
7. **Setup-payoff structure** — rhetorical question, then "the answer lies in...", then a generic explanation. Open with the fact, not a tee-up.

**Why:** Brian flagged my S&W lead paragraph twice (2026-06-17): first the recycled "bottleneck is moving" metaphor, then the "no longer X, it's Y" construction, which he called "tell-tale AI." He asked for a permanent rule. He reads this kind of phrasing as machine-generated and it undercuts credibility with a serious audience.

**How to apply:** Before sending or staging ANY prose, scan for these seven and rewrite any hit. Especially watch #2 — search the text for "no longer," "not just," "not only," "isn't about," "it's about," "rather than simply" used as a rhetorical contrast. Pairs with the S&W voice rules in `agent-prompts/kerri-skill/references/voice-sw-industrialist.md` and the umbrella [[feedback_definition_of_done_gate]]. Confidence comes from the exact fact and a clear declarative sentence, not from formulaic scaffolding.
